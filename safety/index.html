Why we made Oiê

In the 90s, we picked up the home phone and called a friend. We planned the bike ride, the football, the afternoon at someone’s house. The phone was just the way there: the meeting happened outside, for real.

Today, for a 9 to 12-year-old to talk with the people they like, the way there is usually a phone of their own, with feeds, ads and strangers. Or a parent’s phone, lent in a hurry.

Oiê is the home phone again. From the family tablet, your kid sends messages and voice notes to the family and friends you chose. No one else reaches them through Oiê. There are no ads and no feed. And Oiê has no servers: the tablet links to your WhatsApp the way WhatsApp itself does, like WhatsApp Web, and messages go only through WhatsApp. There are no analytics and no tracking. Nothing about your kid reaches us, and no one gathers what they do to sell ads to them.

The idea is the same as before: talk, so you can meet. The rest happens away from the screen.

  • 68% of Brazilian 9 to 17-year-olds who use the internet open WhatsApp every day or almost every day. TIC Kids Online Brasil 2025 (in Portuguese)
  • 30% of 11 and 12-year-olds can tell sponsored content from content that isn’t. TIC Kids Online Brasil 2025 (in Portuguese)

Oiê doesn’t work miracles. No app or setting makes a child 100% safe. What works is the sum: talking, rules agreed as a family, and the tools devices already have. Below is what trusted sources recommend.

Oiê uses your WhatsApp in a way WhatsApp doesn’t officially offer. Read the notice before you link.

Talk first

  1. Little and often, not one big talk

    In calm moments: in the car, while cooking. Ask before you explain: “What was fun online today? Anything weird?”

    UNICEF

  2. Telling is safe

    Make it clear that no one gets punished for telling you something went wrong. If it happens, stay calm.

    UNICEF

  3. Rules agreed together, written down

    When, where and with whom. Adults follow them too: your phone leaves the table as well.

    American Academy of Pediatrics: Family Media Plan

  4. Screen time by age

    From 6 to 10, one to two hours of screens a day, always supervised; from 11 to 18, two to three hours, and never through the night.

    Brazilian Society of Pediatrics (in Portuguese)

  5. Explain the tools you use

    And why. Controls work best in the open, as a partnership; secret monitoring tends to backfire.

    Common Sense Media

  6. Ads and influencers

    Who pays, and why they want your kid’s attention. At 11 and 12, most kids can’t yet tell what’s sponsored.

    TIC Kids Online Brasil 2025 (in Portuguese)

Tools you already have

All of them are free and come from whoever makes the device or the app. None is perfect: their makers say so themselves.

  • Google Family Link

    For your kid’s Android tablet or phone. You approve apps, set screen time and bedtime, see the device’s location and approve Google Play purchases, from your own phone, Android or iPhone. At 13, your kid can turn supervision off: talk about it before the birthday.

    How to set it up: Google Help

  • Google Play, Search and Chrome

    On Google Play, limit apps and games by age rating, behind a PIN. In Google Search, SafeSearch hides explicit results and is locked on for supervised accounts under 13. In supervised Chrome, you can allow only the sites you approve; Google says the filter isn’t perfect.

    Google Play SafeSearch Chrome

  • Samsung Kids

    On Galaxy tablets: a space just for your kid, with the apps and contacts you choose, play time, bedtime, and a PIN to leave it.

    Samsung (menus may differ slightly by country)

  • Apple Screen Time

    If your kid uses an iPad: age ratings, purchase approval, limits on who they can talk to, and downtime. If only you have an iPhone, Family Link works on it to look after your kid’s Android.

    How to set it up: Apple Support

  • YouTube Kids

    Pick content by age, or approve every video and channel yourself. You can turn search off.

    YouTube Kids Help

  • Your WhatsApp

    Oiê uses your WhatsApp, so your settings matter. In Settings › Privacy › Groups, choose who can add you to groups. People you block can’t message or call you.

    Privacy Block and report

  • A filter on your home network

    On your router, a family DNS such as Cloudflare’s 1.1.1.1 for Families (1.1.1.3 and 1.0.0.3) blocks adult sites on every device at home. Mobile data and VPNs go around it.

    Cloudflare

The risks, and what to do

  • Strangers

    Some people pretend to be kids or friends to win a child’s trust. What to do: limit who can talk to your kid, and tell them they can always come to you. On Oiê, only the contacts and groups you approved can talk to them; in a group, anyone not on their list shows marked.

    UNICEF

  • Cyberbullying

    Mean messages, being left out of a group, embarrassing photos. What to do: don’t reply, save screenshots, block, report, and tell an adult.

    UNICEF

  • Sharing too much

    A school uniform, a street name, where they are right now. What to do: agree on what you don’t share, and check together who can see their location.

    UNICEF

  • Scams and purchases

    “Free” coins, fake prizes, games that push spending. What to do: turn on purchase approval (Family Link or Ask to Buy), and don’t leave a card saved on your kid’s device.

    Google Help

  • Ads and data collection

    Many apps record what kids do to show them ads. What to do: prefer apps with no ads and no tracking, and talk about how advertising works. Oiê has no ads.

    Brazil’s data protection authority: ECA Digital (in Portuguese)

  • Too much screen, too little sleep

    Long stretches and late-night use take the place of sleep and play. What to do: keep bedrooms and meals screen-free, especially the hour before bed; devices charge outside the bedroom.

    American Academy of Pediatrics

What Brazil’s law says

  • ECA Digital (Law 15.211/2025)

    In force since 17 March 2026, enforced by Brazil’s data protection authority (ANPD). It forbids collecting children’s and teens’ data to target advertising, and asks for tools that let families follow their kids’ use and settings that prevent compulsive use.

    ANPD: ECA Digital (in Portuguese)

  • LGPD, article 14

    Children’s and teens’ data must be handled in their best interest, and a game or app can’t ask for more data than it needs.

    Law 13.709/2018 (in Portuguese)

  • Children, teens and screens guide (2025)

    The federal government’s guide recommends that children under 12 don’t have a phone of their own, and that schools avoid tasks that depend on messaging apps before that age.

    Secom (in Portuguese)

Where to get help

  • SaferNet’s helpline (Brazil)

    Free guidance by chat or e-mail for kids, teens, parents and teachers: cyberbullying, leaked nudes, dangerous challenges.

    Talk to SaferNet (in Portuguese)

  • Report a crime online (Brazil)

    SaferNet takes anonymous reports. Disque 100 works every day, 24 hours: call 100.

    Call 100 Report to SaferNet About Disque 100 (in Portuguese)

  • Danger right now (Brazil)

    Call 190, the police.

    Call 190

  • In the app where it happened

    Block, report, and save screenshots before deleting anything.

    UNICEF
